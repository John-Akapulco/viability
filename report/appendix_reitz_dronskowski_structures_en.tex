A scope-of-accounting difference, not a shell-detection error.

\noindent\textsuperscript{b}~true first shell only (the 6 basal Zn-Zn bonds at $d=2.608$~\AA); see below for why the dataset's general first-shell detector instead sums 12 bonds here.

\noindent\textsuperscript{c}~Pb's $\sim$2$\times$ residual is present in \emph{both} columns alike (new PBEsol and old PBE), under a bond-summation convention validated to 0.3--0.7\% for every other single-pair-type element in this table (N$_2$, Zn, Sn) --- so it is not a shell-detection or bond-counting artifact of the kind fixed for Zn. A specific alternative hypothesis (LOBSTER double-listing self-image Pb-Pb bonds in this 1-atom primitive cell, flagged by \texttt{reaction\_analysis.parse\_lobster.check\_no\_reverse\_duplicates}) was investigated and \textbf{ruled out} 2026-08-19: that check also flags Zn's already-validated first shell (12/12 records, same pattern), and for a monoatomic cell each flagged pair (e.g. translations $(0,0,-1)$ and $(0,0,1)$) is in fact two \emph{distinct} neighbor images in opposite directions, not the same bond listed twice --- the check's reverse-translation test is a false positive for self-pairs (atom1 = atom2), not a real duplicate. The most plausible remaining explanation is a genuine methodology sensitivity specific to this heavy element (e.g. missing spin-orbit coupling in both this project's calculations, present or implicit in the manuscript's), not yet resolved.

\noindent\textsuperscript{d}~the manuscript's own accounting for S$_4$N$_2$ sums three distinct S-N/S-S bond types explicitly (2 bonds each, $-10.30$/$-8.46$/$-5.86$~eV --- \texttt{tests/fixtures/reitz\_dronskowski\_cases.yaml}); the old-PBE dataset entry's ICOHPLIST resolves only two element-pair labels (S-S, N-S) plus a spurious near-zero N-N artifact, so the three-way split cannot be reconstructed without further structural analysis and is omitted here rather than guessed at.

\noindent\textsuperscript{e}~S$_4$N$_4$'s old-PBE entry uses an unverified COD polymorph (Table~\ref{tab:reitz-dronskowski-structures}: verification ``no''), consistent with a large gap. MnO$_2$ and O$_2$'s $\sim$8--10\% residuals persist under \emph{both} the new PBEsol and old PBE calculations, so are unlikely to be shell-detection artifacts either --- most plausibly a genuine DFT-methodology sensitivity (AFM/Hubbard-U treatment for MnO$_2$, spin-polarization of O$_2$'s triplet ground state) not yet resolved. A two-shell Mn-O sum for MnO$_2$ (mirroring Mn$_2$O$_7$'s explicit short+long convention) was tried and only marginally improves the gap ($8.4\%\to7.2\%$), too small to explain the residual on its own.

Sn and ZnSn were already resolved in the first pass of the 2026-08-18
update: replacing the default $\beta$-tin reference with the
manuscript's $\alpha$-tin phase, computed under its exact methodology,
drops Sn's gap from 38.4\% to 0.4\% --- almost the entire Sn gap was a
polymorph error, not a genuine DFT disagreement --- and excluding the
manuscript-unaccounted Sn-Sn term (note a) drops ZnSn from 8.1\% to
0.2\%.

Zn's 36.0\% residual, left open at the time, was resolved the same day.
Root cause (\texttt{analysis/compute\_manuscript\_validation\_icohp.py}):
\texttt{reaction\_analysis.nearest\_neighbor.detect\_first\_shell()} ---
the general first-shell detector this project uses dataset-wide via
\texttt{bond\_filter="nearest\_neighbor"} for its 588-compound bond-type
classification (\texttt{analysis/compute\_icohp\_icobi\_bondtype.py},
regression-tested in \texttt{tests/test\_nearest\_neighbor.py}) --- cuts
at the single LARGEST gap anywhere in a pair type's whole distance
spectrum, not the first one. Zn's anomalously low hcp $c/a$ ratio puts a
second, chemically distinct Zn-Zn shell (interlayer, $2.729$--$2.734$~\AA)
only $0.12$~\AA\ past the true first shell (basal, $2.608$~\AA) ---
much closer than the $\sim\!1.04$~\AA\ gap to the real third shell ---
so the detector locks onto that later gap and sums both shells (12
bonds instead of 6) as one, roughly doubling the ICOHP magnitude.
Restricting the sum to the true first shell only (note b) gives
$-12.649$~eV, $0.3\%$ from the manuscript's $-12.686$~eV.

This is a genuine limitation of applying one general-purpose
shell-detection rule across a dataset with widely varying coordination
geometries, not a DFT/LOBSTER methodology disagreement --- and it is
\emph{not} evidence against the general detector's own design choice
(the single-largest-gap rule is deliberate and correctly handles the
counter-example that motivated it, see \texttt{nearest\_neighbor.py}'s
docstring). \texttt{nearest\_neighbor.py} itself is left unchanged: Zn's
near-degenerate double shell is not necessarily representative of the
588-compound population, and whether any other compound's bond-type
classification is similarly affected has not been fully closed (see
\S\ref{sec:limits-current}).

\textbf{2026-08-19 extension.} The comparison above was extended from
the original 3 species (ZnSn, Zn, Sn) to all species with a completed
\texttt{manuscript\_*} directory (10/16 at the time of writing, 6 new
since the 2026-08-18 fix: Pb, CaO[sphalerite], CaO[rocksalt], CaN,
MnO$_2$, O$_2$) and, separately, to every species with a pre-existing
PBE-functional dataset entry (14/16 --- all but S$_8$ and
Pb(N$_3$)$_2$, novel to this campaign). Of the 6 newly-added
new-PBEsol comparisons, 3 reproduce the manuscript to within
0.0--1.5\% under the same strict single-shell convention used for Zn
(CaO[sphalerite], CaO[rocksalt], CaN), with no shell-detection
ambiguity found in any of their first-shell distance spectra. Three
residuals remain open rather than force-fit to zero: Pb (note c) and
MnO$_2$/O$_2$ (note e) --- all three investigated, all three ruled out
as instances of the Zn-type shell-detection bug, and left as genuine,
unresolved methodology questions. Mn$_2$O$_7$, S$_4$N$_2$, S$_4$N$_4$,
S$_8$, and Pb(N$_3$)$_2$ remain to be compared once their
\texttt{manuscript\_*} SLURM jobs land.

With ZnSn/Zn/Sn corrected, the recomputed $\Delta$ICOHP for
ZnSn$\to$Zn+Sn ($-3.448$~eV, $-333$~kJ/mol) is within $1.2\%$ of the
manuscript's $-3.489$~eV ($-337$~kJ/mol) --- down from $1.9\times$ before
this correction, and from $2.9\times$ in the original (pre-manuscript-
methodology) calculation. This project's own DFT+LOBSTER ICOHP
methodology reproduces the manuscript's reported values to within
$1.2\%$ for this reaction once (i) the correct polymorphs are used, (ii)
the manuscript's own bond-accounting scope is matched, and (iii)
first-coordination-shell summation is not confounded by near-degenerate
shells --- closing the validation question raised in
\S\ref{sec:reframing} and confirming this project's ICOHP methodology,
not just its arithmetic, matches the manuscript's for the one reaction
where every species is now resolved (\S\ref{sec:reframing}'s own 7-case
validation, using only the manuscript's published values, was never in
question and continues to reproduce them almost exactly).

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Reading key.} ``Verification: Yes'' means the structure was confirmed to match the manuscript's explicit description (a cited space group, or a recomputed bond distance compared directly). ``Partial'' means the geometry/chemistry is consistent with what the manuscript describes but no independent crystallographic citation allowed a strict confirmation. ``No'' means only the composition was verified: the manuscript does not specify a space group for that species, so the most reasonable MP/COD entry was used with no guarantee it is \emph{the} exact structure the authors used. This does not invalidate the \S\ref{sec:reframing} validation (which only compares the arithmetic reconstruction of the manuscript's own published ICOHP values, independent of any local structure) but it does bound the scope of any future comparison between our own DFT+LOBSTER calculations and the manuscript's numbers for these species.
