\section{Objective and Problem Statement}

\textbf{Central research question (see \S\ref{sec:reframing}): does
$\Delta$(ICOHP) --- the reaction-ICOHP descriptor for a compound's
decomposition into elements, and specifically the sign-based
endobondic/exobondic classification it supports --- distinguish
thermodynamically stable, metastable, and unstable compounds?}

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Dataset Selection}

Materials Project query: unary and binary chemical systems,
non-magnetic ($|\text{total magnetization}| \leq 0.01~\mu_B$/formula
unit --- MP's categorical \texttt{magnetic\_ordering} field is
"Unknown" for $\sim$98\% of entries and would have excluded nearly every
genuinely non-magnetic compound), no f-block elements. The dataset
combines two complementary selection tracks: a first set of 186
compounds ($\leq 20$ atoms/cell, 60 per stability family, a
2-entries-per-chemical-system cap to ensure elemental diversity rather
than many polymorphs of the same system), and a second set of 163
compounds/elements ($\leq 40$ atoms/cell) specifically targeting
additional elemental references, off-equilibrium polymorphs
(high-pressure phases, allotropes), and alkali/alkaline-earth binaries
against N/O/F/P/S/Cl, chosen to build genuine same-composition
polymorph groups and to test descriptors far from equilibrium; and a
third set of 45 binary compounds, each pairing an experimentally known,
maximally metastable compound (farthest above the convex hull among
its chemical system) with 1--3 theoretical polymorphs of the same
formula sitting even farther above the hull, a robustness test
deliberately targeting stability edge cases.

\subsection{Bonding-Type Classification}

Bond type (ionic / covalent / metallic) is computed by default by the
\texttt{classify()} function (elemental composition + \texttt{is\_metal}).
A second, independent classification is built directly from each
compound's ICOHP/ICOBI data (\S\ref{sec:dataset}): \texttt{is\_metal}
for metallic character, then the mean ICOBI per bond (bond order,
covalency) to separate ionic from covalent among non-metallic
compounds. The two classifications are compared in \S\ref{sec:dataset}.

\subsection{VASP + LOBSTER Computation}

Same template across all structures (static run, \texttt{ISYM=$-1$},
\texttt{LASPH=.TRUE.}, PAW-PBE potentials recommended by
\texttt{pymatgen.io.vasp.sets.MPRelaxSet}): \texttt{NBANDS} is derived
per compound from the number of local orbitals in the LOBSTER basis
(\texttt{Lobsterin.write\_INCAR}), and basis-function determination goes
through \texttt{Lobsterin.get\_basis(potcar\_symbols=\ldots)} rather than
direct POTCAR-file parsing (whose internal pymatgen validity check fails
on several POTCARs unrecognized by hash in the local mirror).

\subsection{ICOHP/ICOBI Quantities: Integration, Cutoffs, Units}
\label{sec:icohp-methodo}

\textbf{LOBSTER generation (\texttt{lobsterin}, identical across the
dataset)}: \texttt{pbeVaspFit2015} basis; COHP integration window from
$-35$ to $+5$~eV around the Fermi level
(\texttt{COHPstartEnergy}/\texttt{COHPendEnergy}); bond detection by
interatomic distance from 0.1 to 6.0~\AA{} (\texttt{cohpGenerator},
orbital-resolved); 0.05~eV Gaussian smearing
(\texttt{gaussianSmearingWidth}).

\textbf{Integrated ICOHP/ICOBI (\texttt{ICOHPLIST.lobster}/
\texttt{ICOBILIST.lobster}).} A bond's integrated ICOHP is, by LOBSTER's
own definition, the integral of COHP($E$) from the bottom of the window
above up to the Fermi level (occupied states only), in eV; negative =
bonding, positive = antibonding. ICOBI is the same integral over COBI
(bond order index), dimensionless. Per-compound aggregation
(\texttt{percolation\_path.py}): \texttt{icohp\_sum}/\texttt{icobi\_sum}
over every symmetrically inequivalent bond label in
\texttt{ICOHPLIST.lobster}/\texttt{ICOBILIST.lobster} (each periodic bond
counted once, not once per direction); \texttt{icohp\_mean}/
\texttt{icobi\_mean}, the corresponding per-bond average --- this
intensive version, comparable across compounds of different cell sizes,
is what is reported in Table~\ref{tab:bondtype} and the
``ICOHP''/``ICOBI'' columns of
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-master-list}. These quantities feed
\S\ref{sec:dataset} and the classic correlations of \S\ref{sec:results}.

\textbf{Reaction $\Delta$(ICOHP) (\texttt{reaction\_analysis/}).} For a
balanced reaction (typically compound $\to$ elements), $\Delta$ = sum of
products' ICOHP $-$ sum of reactants' ICOHP, "products $-$ reactants"
convention, in three normalizations: per formula unit (extensive), per
transferred atom (the normalization used throughout this report unless
noted), and per bond (diagnostic only, non-conservative --- bonds are
not conserved between differently-bonded structures). Positive sign
("endobondic"): breaking the reactant's bonds costs more than the
products' bonds recover --- a possible kinetic barrier. Negative sign
("exobondic"): no such barrier is visible from bonding alone.

\textbf{Antibonding population near the Fermi level
(\texttt{cohp\_extraction.py}) --- a quantity distinct from the
$\Delta$(ICOHP) above.} Rather than integrating the whole occupied COHP,
this quantity integrates only its antibonding part (COHP$>0$) over a
one-sided window $(E_\text{ref}-\Delta E,\ E_\text{ref}]$, where
$E_\text{ref}=E_F$ (metallic compounds) or the VBM (gapped compounds),
and $\Delta E \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$~eV ($\Delta E=1.0$~eV as the primary
window) --- only occupied states can destabilize the ground state via
antibonding, hence the one-sided rather than symmetric window. Reported
both raw (same "eV" unit convention as LOBSTER, though it is a Hamilton
population integral, not literally an energy) and normalized by the
total occupied ICOHP magnitude at the same $E_\text{ref}$. This
quantity, and only this one, feeds the descriptor in
\S\ref{sec:antibonding} --- the reaction $\Delta$(ICOHP)
(\S\ref{sec:reaction-icohp}--\ref{sec:reframing}) integrates the whole
occupied COHP with no restriction to a window near $E_F$.

\subsection{Statistics}

Spearman correlation (no reason to expect a linear relationship) between
each ICOHP/ICOBI descriptor and a stability target
(\texttt{energy\_above\_hull} or \texttt{formation\_energy\_per\_atom}
depending on the descriptor), overall and per subgroup
(\texttt{bond\_type}, \texttt{is\_metal}); any $n<15$ subgroup is
explicitly flagged, never presented bare. \textbf{No SISSO, no symbolic
regression} at this stage (see \S\ref{sec:limits-current}).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection*{Logic of this section}

Four steps, in this order: (1) validate this project's methodology by
reproducing the 7 reference reactions chosen by Reitz \& Dronskowski;
(2) test this project's central correlation --- viability (a compound's
thermodynamic/kinetic stability) against descriptors derived from LOBSTER
data (ICOHP, antibonding ICOHP, ICOBI, antibonding ICOBI); (3) classify
compounds and elements into metallic/ionic/covalent/mixed directly from
that same ICOHP/ICOBI data, and compare that classification against
\texttt{classify()} --- a tool in service of the next step, not an end in
itself; (4) determine whether step 2's correlation is strengthened or
weakened once conditioned on step 3's bond type.

The 7 cases reproduce the reference $\Delta$ICOHP to within a few kJ/mol
(source per-bond value rounding) and every \texttt{BondingLabel}
matches. The Mn$_2$O$_7$ case is a useful guardrail on its own: exobondic
under this static sign criterion, yet Mn$_2$O$_7$ is a real, observed
compound that simply decomposes slowly (gradual O$_2$ loss) rather than
through abrupt bond breaking --- \texttt{classify.py} structurally
attaches a warning to \emph{every} \texttt{UNSTABLE\_NONEXISTENT} verdict
for exactly this reason, rather than special-casing Mn$_2$O$_7$.

This validation only compares the arithmetic of the manuscript's own
published ICOHP values, independent of any local crystal structure ---
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-reitz-dronskowski-structures} documents, for
the 16 species involved, which MP/COD structure was used in this dataset
and whether it could be verified to match the manuscript's description
(3 real corrections identified: N$_2$, the Pb(N$_3$)$_2$ polymorph,
ZnSn's competing tin phase). For N$_2$ and ZnSn, a DFT+LOBSTER
calculation of this project's own (not just the manuscript's arithmetic)
was additionally compared directly against the manuscript's numbers
(Table~\ref{tab:reitz-dronskowski-dft}): N$_2$ reproduces the
manuscript's ICOHP to within 0.7\%; ZnSn reproduces the same sign
(bonding-favorable decomposition) but with $\sim$2.9$\times$ larger
magnitude --- a gap judged not significant at this stage, since a bulk
intermetallic's ICOHP sum is far more sensitive to
basis/pseudopotential/cutoff choices than a simple gas dimer. An
independent campaign reproducing the manuscript's full DFT methodology
(PBEsol+D3, Table S1's own k-point sets) across all 16 species, rather
than just these two, is under way (\S\ref{sec:limits-current}).

Correlation alone (Pearson/Spearman) is not the right test for "do our
numbers match the reference values" --- it is satisfied by any monotonic
relationship, even with a large systematic offset or scale factor.
\textbf{Lin's concordance correlation coefficient} (CCC) specifically
tests agreement with the identity line (computed = reference), the claim
actually being tested here:

\textbf{Antibonding population near the Fermi level (ICOHP).}
\label{sec:antibonding}
A distinct
question from the fully-integrated ICOHP (\S2.4): not \emph{how much}
bonding a compound has, but \emph{how} COHP is distributed in energy ---
the occupied antibonding fraction just below the Fermi level/VBM, by
analogy with Peierls/Jahn-Teller-type electronic instabilities. Result,
against \texttt{formation\_energy\_per\_atom}: $\rho=-0.155$,
$p=1.8\times10^{-4}$ ($n=579$) --- weaker than at smaller scale
($\rho=-0.282$, $n=346$) but still real and significant; the sign
remains the \emph{opposite} of the naive Peierls/Jahn-Teller reading
(\S\ref{sec:stratification} for the bond-type-stratified reading, where
this descriptor's headline stratified result no longer survives at the
current scale).

\textbf{Reaction ICOHP.}
\label{sec:reaction-icohp}
An ICOHP analog of
\texttt{formation\_energy\_per\_atom}: is a compound's total ICOHP
"cheaper" than that of the same atoms as reference elements? Result:
$\rho=0.295$, $p\approx0$ ($n=510$) --- a real, highly significant

unambiguously \emph{the} strongest. Case 2 (direct same-formula
polymorph comparison, 74 groups): 47.3\% agreement between the
most-bonded and the most-stable member, against a 42.4\% chance
baseline (exact Poisson-binomial test, $P=0.22$) --- no signal.

\textbf{Reading trap, explicitly flagged by \texttt{classify.py} itself}:
Table~\ref{tab:fisher} shows 188 \emph{experimental} (i.e. genuinely
existing) compounds labeled \emph{exobondic}. This is \textbf{not} a
contradiction --- exobondic only implies "not viable" once combined with
\texttt{delta\_energy} (\texttt{classify\_viability()}): a compound is
only \texttt{UNSTABLE\_NONEXISTENT} if decomposition into elements is
\emph{both} exobondic \emph{and} thermodynamically favorable
($\Delta E<0$), which is rare (decomposing a real compound into pure
elements is almost never favorable). Verified directly on the same
population as Table~\ref{tab:fisher}:

\subsection{Step 3 --- Bond-type classification from ICOHP/ICOBI data}
\label{sec:bondtype-classification}

A tool in service of the next step, not an end in itself: this
classification exists solely to test, in step 4, whether step 2's
viability-descriptor correlation depends on a compound's bond type. Two
independent classifications (\S2.2): the \texttt{classify()} function
(elemental composition + \texttt{is\_metal}) and a classification built
directly from each compound's ICOHP/ICOBI data (\texttt{is\_metal} for
metallic character; otherwise the ICOBI of the \textbf{dominant
first-coordination-shell} species pair --- not a mean over every
neighbor LOBSTER reports within a wider cutoff, which strongly dilutes
the signal for dense covalent solids such as diamond --- compared
against a threshold calibrated at the midpoint of the median ICOBI of
compounds \texttt{classify()} already labels ionic/covalent, i.e.
0.5315, to separate ionic from covalent among non-metallic compounds).
A fourth label, \textbf{mixed} (Zintl-type), identifies compounds where
a \emph{homoatomic} pair above the covalent threshold (e.g. N--N in an
azide, P--P in a polyphosphide, S--S in a polysulfide) coexists with a
markedly weaker \emph{heteroatomic} pair linking a distinct cation ---
the structural signature of Zintl--Klemm chemistry, where a single
ionic/covalent label would be misleading.

\subsection{Step 4 --- Does the viability-descriptor correlation depend on bond type?}
\label{sec:stratification}

\textbf{Antibonding population (ICOHP).} \textbf{The project's previously
most robust result is retracted}: \texttt{bond\_type=covalent}, which
survived clearly at $n=33$ ($\rho=-0.551$, $p=0.0009$), no longer
survives once the sample grew to $n=54$ ($\rho=-0.046$, $p=0.74$) --- a
failure mode already seen elsewhere in this project (a subgroup result
that does not replicate once the sample grows), including this same
descriptor's own earlier \texttt{bond\_type=ionic} subgroup.
\textbf{New at this scale}: \texttt{bond\_type=mixed} (Zintl) is now the
best-surviving stratum ($\rho=-0.538$, $p=6.6\times10^{-6}$, $n=62$) ---
\textbf{strengthened} relative to step 2's pooled correlation --- a
category that barely existed when the covalent result was first
reported. \texttt{is\_metal=False} remains the most consistently
surviving stratum across this project's successive scales
($\rho=-0.333$, $p=6.7\times10^{-7}$, $n=212$) --- \textbf{strengthened},
not weakened, by the dataset's growth. \texttt{is\_metal=True} has also
converged onto \texttt{bond\_type=metallic} (identical $n=367$,
$\rho=0.056$, not significant), the same phenomenon documented below
for reaction ICOHP.

\textbf{Reaction ICOHP.} \texttt{bond\_type} stratification (now four
categories, including \texttt{mixed}/Zintl) no longer uniformly dilutes
step 2's pooled correlation ($\rho=0.295$): \texttt{ionic} ($\rho=0.360$,
$p=0.0004$) and \texttt{mixed} ($\rho=-0.525$, opposite sign,
$p\approx0$) both survive clearly --- \textbf{strengthened} in
magnitude --- \texttt{covalent} too but flips sign ($\rho=-0.354$,
$p=0.023$); only \texttt{metallic} (the majority class, 315/510 rows)
stays non-significant --- \textbf{weakened} to the point of
disappearing in that stratum. \textbf{\texttt{is\_metal} stratification
--- the one that used to survive --- has now converged onto the same
population as \texttt{bond\_type=metallic}} (identical $n$, $\rho$, $p$
in every row) and so inherits its non-significant result: it is
\texttt{bond\_type}, not \texttt{is\_metal}, that carries information at
the current dataset composition. Against \texttt{energy\_above\_hull},
\texttt{bond\_type=mixed} again survives on its own ($\rho=-0.307$,
$p=0.016$).

\textbf{Synthesis.} In both cases (antibonding and reaction ICOHP), this

merely weaken or strengthen a single global correlation, it changes the
\emph{sign} of the relationship depending on the stratum (ionic
positive, covalent/mixed negative for reaction ICOHP) --- the useful
signal is now conditional on bond type. The antibonding population's
covalent-result retraction is itself a direct illustration of why this
step exists: a stratified number is only trustworthy once re-checked as
the dataset grows, not accepted once and for all. A pooled feature
search (SISSO) would therefore be actively misleading here; any future
search should be run separately per \texttt{bond\_type}, not on the
pooled population.

\section{Failures Found and Fixed}
\label{sec:incidents}

Per the instruction ("every job failure must be logged with its probable
cause"), 5 of the first 178 SLURM-array-submitted jobs failed on the
first pass, all diagnosed and fixed at the source (the fixes, applied to
the shared SLURM/INCAR templates, carry over to the whole dataset):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Al$_{12}$W, BW, WO$_2$, TcW, TiW} (all tungsten-containing):
    the local mirror's \texttt{W\_sv} POTCAR is missing its \texttt{LEXCH}

    other variant checked, including plain \texttt{W}), which VASP reads
    as an incompatible exchange-correlation functional and refuses to run.
    Fixed by replacing the \texttt{W\_sv} override with plain \texttt{W}
    (valid PBE, verified).
  \item \textbf{Al$_{12}$W} failed again after that fix, this time with a
    VASP SIGSEGV --- a known issue
    (\texttt{ulimit -s unlimited} before \texttt{vasp\_std}), present in
    \texttt{submit\_array.sh} but forgotten in
    \texttt{prepare\_vasp\_lobster.py}'s per-compound SLURM template.
    Fixed; recovered on retry.
\end{itemize}

Final success rate across all 597 structures computed to date:
\textbf{597/597} for VASP; \textbf{596/597} for LOBSTER (1 trivial-cell
reference element with no usable LOBSTER step, \S\ref{sec:cost}).

\section{Current Limits and Next Steps}
\label{sec:limits-current}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One compound excluded for LOBSTER quality}:
    \texttt{extension\_Cu4Au\_mp-1225761} was dropped from every
    analysis (but not from the raw dataset -- its VASP+LOBSTER
    calculation is kept for reference) --- \texttt{lobsterout} reports
    343/343 k-points failed orthonormalization (100\%, not a borderline
    case), and \texttt{bandOverlaps.lobster} shows a maximum deviation
    of 11.4 (this project's own established red-flag threshold is 0.1).
    Direct consequence: physically implausible ICOHP values (third-
    neighbor Cu--Cu contacts at 4.25--5.04~\AA{} with ICOHP values of
    7.6--11.6~eV) and a $\Delta$ICOHP/atom of $-380.5$~eV,
    $\sim$27$\times$ the next-worst case in the rest of the population.
    A systematic sweep of all 597 structures
    (\texttt{analysis/audit\_lobster\_quality.py}, criterion: 100\%
    k-point failure \emph{and} deviation $>0.5$ \emph{and} robust
    z-score $>10$) confirms this is a unique case, not a wider problem
    --- the second-worst case (\texttt{Ir4W}) is already 29$\times$ less
    extreme and plausibly real chemistry (a dense, d-electron-rich
    intermetallic). Every statistic in this report covering the 518
    case-1 reactions has been regenerated on the remaining 517 ---
    the effect on any given number is negligible (3rd--4th decimal
    place), no conclusion or significance threshold is affected.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{classify()} coverage}: 277/588 compounds (47\%)
    fall outside the ionic/covalent/metallic categories, mostly
    alkali/alkaline-earth $\times$ \{N, P, S\} bonds that
    \texttt{IONIC\_ANIONS} does not recognize (\S\ref{sec:dataset}) ---
    purely informative here, but a real limitation for any mission that
    would want to stratify on it.
  \item \textbf{No true dynamical "unstable" class}: \texttt{theo\_metastable}
    is a proxy (never synthesized), not a phonon-calculation result.
  \item \textbf{Per-subgroup sample size}: remains this project's
    structural limit at every new stratification (\texttt{bond\_type},
    \texttt{is\_metal}) --- see \S\ref{sec:antibonding} for a concrete
    example where an $n<15$ subgroup did not replicate once the sample
    grew.
  \item \textbf{Why not SISSO yet}: reaction ICOHP's signal
    (\S\ref{sec:reaction-icohp}) is now conditional on bond type and
    opposite-signed across strata (ionic positive, covalent/mixed
    negative) --- a pooled feature search would be misleading here; any
    future search should be run separately per \texttt{bond\_type}.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{family} not yet extended to the newer batches}:
    the \texttt{theoretical}-based \texttt{ViabilityLabel}/Fisher
    discrimination (\S\ref{sec:reframing}) fully benefits from the
    dataset's growth, but the finer three-way split
    (\texttt{exp\_metastable}/\texttt{exp\_stable}/\texttt{theo\_metastable})
    stays at $n=177$ for lack of a \texttt{family} label on the
    extension/marginal-formation-energy/max-hull batches --- the direct
    next step to test whether that finer split also turns significant.
  \item \textbf{The \texttt{ViabilityLabel} result (\S\ref{sec:reframing},
    $p=2.9\times10^{-15}$) has not yet been checked for confounds}:
    neither stratified by \texttt{bond\_type}/\texttt{is\_metal}, nor
    distinguished from a selection effect (max-hull's theoretical
    polymorphs were deliberately chosen far above the hull).
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

Reitz \& Dronskowski's endobondic/exobondic methodology
(\S\ref{sec:reframing}) reproduces faithfully across the 7 independently
known reference reactions (Lin's CCC 0.999998) and applies to this
report's full dataset (518 decomposition-into-elements reactions). On
that population, sign-based $\Delta$ICOHP discrimination is now
significant (Fisher's test, $p=0.0054$), and the full
\texttt{ViabilityLabel} machinery --- sign combined with thermodynamics
--- is far more strongly so ($p=2.9\times10^{-15}$, odds ratio 6.0): an
experimentally realized compound is roughly 6$\times$ less likely to be
predicted \texttt{UNSTABLE\_NONEXISTENT} than a theoretical-only one
(\S\ref{sec:reframing}). Reaction ICOHP otherwise remains a strong
continuous correlate against formation energy ($\rho=0.295$,
\S\ref{sec:reaction-icohp}), though antibonding population now slightly
edges it out on the subset shared by both descriptors.

Two concrete directions follow. First, checking whether the
\texttt{ViabilityLabel} result above survives stratification by
\texttt{bond\_type}/\texttt{is\_metal} and is not simply carried by
max-hull's deliberate selection effect --- the same verification
discipline this project applies systematically to every new descriptor.
Second, restricting the reaction $\Delta$(ICOHP) to a window near the
Fermi level rather than the fully integrated ICOHP, modeled on the
antibonding population of \S\ref{sec:antibonding}: two variants of this
construction are detailed in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-icobi-antibonding} (antibonding part only,
and the full signed ICOHP restricted to the window) --- the first
survives every stratification tested, a result no other descriptor in
this report reaches.

\subsection{Implementation}

The ICOHP near-Fermi-level antibonding-population descriptor
(\S\ref{sec:antibonding}, \texttt{cohp\_extraction.py}) is extended
here to ICOBI: same one-sided window
$(E_\text{ref}-\Delta E,\ E_\text{ref}]$, same $E_\text{ref}$ (Fermi
level for metals, VBM for gapped compounds --- logic entirely
independent of the integrated quantity), read this time from
\texttt{COBICAR.lobster}/\texttt{ICOBILIST.lobster} rather than
\texttt{COHPCAR.lobster}/\texttt{ICOHPLIST.lobster}
(\texttt{pymatgen.io.lobster.outputs.Cohpcar(are\_cobis=True)}).

\textbf{Critical point, empirically verified, not assumed}: ICOBI does
\textbf{not} follow ICOHP's sign convention. Whereas \og{}negative
ICOHP = bonding\fg{} (\S2.4), ICOBI follows the opposite convention,
\og{}positive ICOBI = bonding\fg{} (like COOP, not COHP). Verified
twice against real data: (1) exact match with
\texttt{ICOBILIST.lobster} (maximum deviation 0.0 across 66 bond
labels, \texttt{tests/test\_cohp\_extraction.py}) --- \texttt{pymatgen}
does not silently renormalize the sign when moving from COHP to COBI;
(2) on the strongest Si--O bond of \texttt{extension\_SiO2\_anchor}
(ICOHP $=-5.599$, ICOBI $=+0.513$), the deep bonding region ($-10$ to
$-3$~eV, far from any boundary artifact) gives a mean COHP($E$) of
$-0.338$ but a mean COBI($E$) of $+0.041$ --- opposite signs in the
same physically-bonding region. The antibonding part of ICOBI is
therefore its \emph{negative} part, not its positive part --- the
truncation direction
(\texttt{integrate\_icobi\_antibonding\_in\_window()}) is deliberately
the mirror image of the one used for ICOHP, not a copy of it.

Computed for the 588 compounds with both a \texttt{COBICAR.lobster}
and a \texttt{COHPCAR.lobster}, \texttt{is\_metal} available for all of
them (\texttt{analysis/compute\_icobi\_antibonding\_all.py},
\texttt{analysis/compute\_antibonding\_all\_full.py} --- one compound,
\texttt{Cu4Au}, excluded for a catastrophic LOBSTER-projection failure,
\S\ref{sec:limites-actuelles}).

\textbf{The two descriptors still behave differently, and
\emph{neither} keeps its former flagship stratified result.} The
overall sign of the ICOBI antibonding population remains
\emph{opposite} to the ICOHP antibonding population ($+0.23$ vs.

counter-intuitive in \S\ref{sec:antibonding}); for ICOBI, the

subgroup of the ICOHP antibonding population, long the project's most
robust stratified result ($\rho=-0.551$ at $n=33$), no longer survives

\texttt{bond\_type=mixed} (Zintl) now dominates the ICOHP antibonding

clustering effect rather than a robust within-group relationship.

\subsection{$\Delta$(antibonding) of the decomposition-into-elements reaction}

For a case-1 reaction (compound $\to$ elements), $\Delta$(antibonding)
is \textbf{not} computed by \texttt{reaction\_analysis/delta.py} (which
combines \emph{extensive} per-formula-unit ICOHP/ICOBI sums with
integer coefficients): the near-frontier antibonding population is
built from LOBSTER's \og{}average\fg{} trace, an \emph{intensive}
quantity (averaged over all bonds, not summed) with no natural
extensive decomposition. Convention adopted here, documented as an
explicit methodological choice rather than a silent reuse: $\Delta$ =
(atomic-fraction-weighted average of the elements' antibonding
descriptors) $-$ (the compound's own antibonding descriptor), always
in the products $-$ reactants convention. Computed for 439 case-1
reactions (\texttt{analysis/compute\_delta\_antibonding\_case1.py}).

\subsection{Reaction $\Delta$(ICOHP) restricted to a window near $E_F$}
\label{sec:reaction-icohp-windowed}

Extension proposed in the conclusion (\S\ref{sec:reframing}): rather
than the fully-integrated ICOHP over the whole occupied range
(\S\ref{sec:reaction-icohp}) or only the antibonding part near $E_F$
(previous subsections), a third construction --- the \emph{signed}
ICOHP (bonding \emph{and} antibonding, not clipped) but integrated
\emph{only} over the one-sided window $(E_F-\Delta E,\ E_F]$,
$\Delta E=1.0$~eV, summed over \emph{every} bond label (extensive, like
the full ICOHP sum) rather than the \og{}average\fg{} trace --- then
combined with the same reaction stoichiometry as the full
$\Delta$(ICOHP) (\texttt{reaction\_icohp.py}, reactant $-$ predicted
products convention). Implementation:
\texttt{cohp\_extraction.icohp\_windowed\_per\_atom()} reads directly
the difference between two points on the \emph{cumulative} ICOHP curve
LOBSTER already computes in \texttt{COHPCAR.lobster}
(\texttt{ICOHP\_cumulative}$(E_F)$ $-$
\texttt{ICOHP\_cumulative}$(E_F-\Delta E)$, per bond label), with no
new trapezoidal integration --- validated: at very large $\Delta E$
(covering the whole computed range), this quantity recovers the full
ICOHP sum to within $\sim$0.1\%. Computed for 439 case-1 reactions.
